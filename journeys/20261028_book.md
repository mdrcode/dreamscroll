## Current recommendation

**Primary: Patrick Modiano, *Dora Bruder*.**

It is the closest thing to Sebald without being Sebald: a short archival
investigation into a missing Jewish girl in occupied Paris, blending
documentary evidence, memoir, and speculation. It has the *Austerlitz* /
*The Emigrants* obsession with disappearance and the limits of reconstruction,
but at roughly 150 pages it is a concentrated read rather than a maximalist
project. Modiano is already in the corpus at #158 (*The Occupation Trilogy*),
so this extends an existing thread rather than introducing a new one.

**Open question:** stay in the documentary/archival mode with Modiano, move
toward the more visceral displacement of Herta Müller, or take the digressive
Central European travelogue route with Claudio Magris.

**American primary: Denis Johnson, *Train Dreams*.**

A compressed life of Robert Grainier, an early-20th-century laborer in the
Pacific Northwest, built from work, railroad, fire, and loss. It is American,
elegiac, and formally quiet—the closest American equivalent to the mode of
*The Emigrants* without imitating Sebald. Short enough to read in a sitting or
two.
